How storms get water into South Gate homes

Los Angeles area storms are infrequent but can be intense, so many homes here have never had their drainage tested by real rain. A downspout that ends at the foundation or a driveway that slopes toward the garage shows its flaw only when a hard storm arrives.

Wind drives rain sideways through window frames and door seams. Runoff pools against thresholds and garage doors, and a fallen branch can puncture a roof. Each route wets a different part of the house.

Safety before you step into storm water

Storm water can carry mud, debris and whatever runoff picked up on the street, and it can hide electrical hazards.

Power, gas and downed lines

Do not wade into water that touches outlets, appliances, a panel or wiring. Cut power only if the panel is dry and you can reach it without stepping through water. For a gas smell, sparking or a downed line, call the utility or 911 first, then call us.

Keep people out from under a bulging ceiling and children and pets away from standing water.

When a storm loss turns into a flood loss

A storm loss is rain that comes through an opening in the building, such as a window, a roof or a door seal. A flood loss is surface water from outside that rises and enters, often across a whole floor and carrying mud. Our flood damage restoration page covers rising flood water.

Insurers treat the two differently. A garage that fills from the street moves your loss toward flood, even if it began as an ordinary storm. Flood is usually excluded from homeowners policies and needs separate flood coverage.

Extraction and drying after the storm

Moisture meters read walls and framing, and a thermal imaging camera shows wet areas behind finishes. The ANSI/IICRC S500 standard is the written industry guide for restoring water-damaged structures, and you can ask any company how it applies it.

Air movers and LGR dehumidifiers run on floors and walls.

Check the readings

Return visits show when materials reach the dry goal.

Before the next storm: drainage and sandbag basics

Walk the property before the rainy season

  • ✓Clear gutters and downspouts
  • ✓Direct downspout outlets away from the foundation
  • ✓Check garage door seals and window caulk

Sandbags slow shallow runoff at a door or garage threshold, but they do not seal. Fill them about half full and stack them in a staggered pattern in front of the opening. They are no help against rising flood water.
