Renters Insurance and Water Damage: What Tenants Should Know

A renters policy usually covers your belongings and extra living costs after sudden water damage, while the landlord's policy covers the building. Flood and slow leaks are the usual gaps. Check your own policy.

☎ Call (213) 776-0579

By the South Gate Water Damage Pros team · 2026

If a pipe bursts or a neighbor's unit leaks into yours, a renters policy usually pays for sudden and accidental damage to your belongings. It often adds money for a hotel or extra living costs if you cannot stay home. It does not usually pay to repair the building itself, because the landlord insures that. Flood, slow leaks and neglect are commonly excluded.

Policies differ, so treat this page as general background and read your own declarations page. Your insurer decides what is covered, and the California Department of Insurance consumer hotline at 1-800-927-4357 can answer general questions.

Myths and facts about renters coverage

Common assumptions, checked
What people sayWhat is usually true
My landlord's insurance covers my furnitureThe landlord's policy typically covers the building. Your belongings need your own policy.
Any water damage is coveredSudden and accidental events are often covered. Gradual leaks and neglect often are not.
Renters insurance covers floodsHomeowners and renters policies commonly exclude flood. Flood coverage is a separate policy.
If the building is damaged, I owe nothingIf your own carelessness caused it, you may be responsible for part of the cost.
I can wait and see if it dries on its ownPolicies usually expect you to take reasonable steps to stop further damage.

That last row is the one that costs tenants money. Soaked carpet, drywall and furniture can grow mold in about a day or two, and waiting can turn a smaller claim into a larger dispute.

What a renters policy usually pays for

  • Personal property: furniture, clothing, electronics and appliances you own, damaged by a covered event such as a burst supply line.
  • Loss of use: costs for a place to stay and extra food costs while the unit is unlivable. Limits and time caps vary.
  • Personal liability: if water from your unit damages a neighbor's unit, this part may help with their claim against you. An overflowing tub is the classic example.
  • Deductible: you pay this first. A small loss may fall below it, so know your number before filing.

Sewer backup and water from outside the home often need an added endorsement. Look for those words on your policy or ask your agent whether you have them.

Written notice matters more than a phone call. Send a text or email with the date, photos and a short description, then keep the thread. If it becomes a dispute, a tenant rights organization or an attorney can advise you, because this is a legal question and not one a cleanup company can settle.

Your first 24 hours as a tenant

1

Stop the source

Close the shutoff valve under the sink or toilet, or the unit's main. If the water is coming from above, tell the neighbor and the landlord.

2

Stay clear of electricity

If water is near outlets or a panel, do not touch them. Cut power only if the panel is dry and safe to reach, and call the utility or 911 for electrical or gas risk.

3

Photograph everything

Take wide shots and close-ups before moving anything, including the ceiling stain, floor, and the make and model of damaged items.

4

Notify in writing

Email or text your landlord and property manager the same day, then follow up by phone.

5

Call your insurer

Ask about your deductible, loss of use limits, and whether they want an adjuster to see items before you discard them.

6

Start drying

Pulling wet pad, running air movers and setting a dehumidifier cuts the spread. Keep every receipt.

Flood, sewer and the gaps people discover late

California insurance guidance says homeowners policies typically exclude flood, mudslide and debris flow. Renters policies often follow the same pattern. Flood coverage comes from the National Flood Insurance Program, and renters can buy it for their belongings even if they do not own the building. The NFIP claims line is 1-800-638-6620.

If the water came from outside, such as street runoff, or from a sewer line, the claim route changes. Large losses may need flood damage restoration crews with different safety steps, and sewage means you should stay out of the area.

When the landlord does not respond

Keep your records, send a second written notice, and ask your insurer whether your policy can help with temporary housing. For active leaks, you can also call for emergency water removal yourself so the damage does not spread while the dispute continues, then share the invoice and readings.
