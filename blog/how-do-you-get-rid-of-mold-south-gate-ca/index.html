How Do You Get Rid of Mold in Your Home?

You get rid of household mold by fixing the moisture source first, then containing the area, removing or cleaning affected materials, drying everything, and checking the result before rebuilding.

☎ Call (213) 776-0579

By the South Gate Water Damage Pros team · 2026

Getting rid of mold in a home is a sequence, and the order matters. First fix the water problem, because mold needs moisture. Then contain the affected area, remove or clean what is contaminated, dry the space, and verify the work before anything is rebuilt. Skipping the first step or the last one is where most failed jobs go wrong.

This page covers the whole-home process. For one small piece of wood, see the narrower steps for cleaning surface growth, and for large or hidden growth plan on professional help.

The sequence from wet to clear

1

Stop the moisture

Repair the leak, fix the roof, vent the bathroom or drain the crawl space. Nothing else works while water keeps arriving.

2

Assess the extent

A crew uses moisture meters and a thermal imaging camera to find wet areas behind surfaces, not only visible spots.

3

Contain the area

Plastic barriers and negative air keep disturbed material from drifting into clean rooms.

4

Remove and clean

Porous materials like drywall, ceiling tile and carpet pad are cut out and bagged. Solid materials are HEPA vacuumed and wiped.

5

Dry thoroughly

Air movers and LGR dehumidifiers bring materials back to normal moisture readings.

6

Verify and rebuild

The area is checked before finishes go back. Then drywall, flooring and paint are replaced.

Professional mold remediation follows ANSI/IICRC S520, the published standard for professional mold remediation. You can ask any company whether its crews work to it and what licenses it holds, then look the license up on the California Contractors State License Board website.

Step one is always the water

If a leak, a failed wax ring, a clogged condensate line or a roof gap is still active, cleaning only buys time. A plumber, roofer or HVAC technician fixes the source. Then the wet materials must dry, and the EPA says to dry or discard wet materials within 24 to 48 hours to limit growth.

That window is why a recent leak calls for water mitigation before anything else. Drying a wall cavity that has been wet for a day is far easier than removing a wall that has been wet for a month.

What gets cleaned and what gets removed

Material decisions in a typical job
MaterialUsual decisionWhy
Drywall with growthRemove and replacePaper facing and gypsum are porous and hard to clean
Insulation, carpet pad, ceiling tileRemove and discardThey hold moisture and cannot be cleaned reliably
Framing and subfloor, solidHEPA vacuum, clean, drySolid wood can often stay if it is sound
Concrete, tile, metal, glassClean and dryNon-porous or hard surfaces clean well
Upholstery and clothingCase by caseWashable items may be saved, soft furniture often is not

These are general patterns. A crew makes final decisions on site from the readings and what they see once surfaces are opened. Color of the growth does not identify a species or tell you how serious the job is.

Containment and air control

Removal stirs up dust and spores, so a good crew isolates the work area with sheeting and a negative air machine with HEPA filtration. Doors, vents and returns are sealed, and workers enter through a controlled opening. Without this step, a small job in one room can become a whole-house cleanup.

Ask the company how the HVAC system will be handled. If the air handler or ducts pull from the affected room, that needs to be addressed in the plan. Never run a fan that pulls air out of the work area into the rest of the house.

Clearance: how you know the job is finished

After removal and drying, the area should be inspected before rebuilding. That means visual checks, moisture readings at dry levels, and no musty smell. Some jobs also include post-remediation testing by a separate third party, which avoids a conflict of interest when the same company does the work and the verification.

Before you approve the rebuild

  • ✓The moisture source has been repaired and documented
  • ✓Moisture readings are at normal levels across the affected area
  • ✓Affected materials were removed, bagged and taken away
  • ✓The area was HEPA vacuumed and wiped
  • ✓Containment came down only after inspection
  • ✓You have the written scope, photos and final readings

No honest company can promise mold will never return, because a future leak can create new growth. What keeps it away is a dry building and quick action when something gets wet.

Permits, drying time and South Gate homes

The South Gate Building and Safety Division at 8650 California Avenue, (323) 563-9549, says permits are typically required for repair, alteration and construction, including plumbing, mechanical and electrical work. If mold removal opens walls and you then replace framing, wiring or pipes, ask the division what applies before the rebuild begins, so the schedule is not stalled.

Many South Gate homes date from the early to mid twentieth century, when much of the old farmland was built over. Older plaster, lath and paint can be present in walls that mold has affected. Tell the crew your home's age and ask about testing those materials before demolition.

Insurance may cover mold that follows a sudden and accidental water loss, while mold from long-term neglect often is not, so check your policy.

When you can do it yourself and when you cannot

A small patch on a hard surface in a well-ventilated space is a reasonable DIY job with a respirator, gloves and goggles. Anything on drywall, in a ceiling, behind a wall, near an air handler or after sewage should go to a mold remediation professional.
