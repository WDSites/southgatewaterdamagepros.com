Water Damage on Hardwood Floors: The First 48 Hours

Hardwood absorbs water through its edges and underside, so the hours after a spill decide whether boards flatten out or stay warped. Here is a timeline of what to do, what to skip, and what drying involves.

By the South Gate Water Damage Pros team · 2026

In the first hour, stop the water, remove standing water and open the room to airflow. Within the first day, pull up rugs, move furniture and get moisture readings from the boards and the subfloor. By hour 48, you should know whether the floor is drying back toward normal or heading into cupping and mold risk. Speed matters more than any product you can buy.

Why hardwood cups, crowns and buckles

Wood swells as it takes on water, and it takes on water faster through the underside and edges than through the finish. When the bottom of a board holds more moisture than the top, the edges rise and the board cups. If the top dries slower than the bottom, the center rises instead and you get crowning.

Buckling is the severe end. Boards lift off the subfloor, often because water sat for days. Cupping that shows up a day or two after a leak does not mean the floor is ruined. Many floors flatten out as they dry slowly, which is why sanding too early is a mistake. Give the wood time to settle before judging it.

Not every wet floor is equal. A dishwasher overflow that is mopped up within an hour is a different problem from a slab-side leak that soaked the underlayment for a weekend. Water type, time and board thickness all change the outcome, which is why photos and readings matter more than guesses.

Safety before anything else

Check electricity and water type

If water reached outlets, baseboard heaters or an appliance, keep out and cut power only if the panel is dry and safe to reach. For gas smells or sparks, leave and call 911 or the utility first. Sewage or a backed-up drain means the water is unsafe to touch, so wait for professional help.

What you can do yourself while you wait

Safe steps in the first few hours

  • ✓Shut off the water at the fixture valve or main
  • ✓Remove standing water with a wet vacuum or mop and towels
  • ✓Lift area rugs, mats and furniture legs off the floor, using foil or plastic blocks under legs
  • ✓Wipe surface water off boards and run a fan across the floor
  • ✓Take photos and video of the source, the damage
  • ✓Write down the time you found the water
  • ✓Keep wet items, since your insurer may want to see them

Move your own belongings first. Pull rugs outside, since the backing can stain boards, and lift the legs of heavy furniture onto foil or plastic blocks. If the water came from a toilet, washing machine hose or supply line, mention that to the crew because it affects the cleanup plan under water damage cleanup methods.

Do not seal the floor with plastic or a water-based finish. A sealed surface traps moisture in the board and can turn a recoverable floor into a lost one.

How professional drying works on hardwood

A crew uses a moisture meter to read both the boards and the subfloor underneath. Readings guide everything: how many air movers, whether to add an LGR dehumidifier and whether the floor needs special drying mats. Mats pull moisture up through the boards by applying suction to the surface.

Drying hardwood takes patience. Pushing heat too hard can split boards and leave gaps. The standard is gradual drying until readings match a dry reference. Learn more about this stage on our water mitigation page.

EPA mold guidance says to dry or discard wet materials within 24 to 48 hours to limit mold growth. That window is the reason the clock on this page runs in hours rather than days.

When to call a pro and what happens next

Call as soon as water has touched more than a small area, soaked under the boards or lasted more than a few hours. A crew confirms the source is stopped, removes standing water and maps the wet area. For a burst line or overflow, emergency water removal is usually the first stage.

Once drying is verified, the floor is assessed for repair or replacement. That decision is covered in a separate guide, but the short version is that stable moisture readings come first and sanding comes last. Nearby homeowners in Paramount and Downey get the same process.
