Basement Flood Cleanup Services: What Happens and What to Do First

Flood cleanup in a below-grade space means safe pumping, removing contaminated materials, drying and sanitizing. Here is what each step involves and what to do before help arrives.

☎ Call (213) 776-0579

By the South Gate Water Damage Pros team · 2026

Flood cleanup services for a basement, below-grade garage or crawl space do five things: make the space safe, pump out the water, remove materials that cannot be saved, dry and sanitize what remains, and document the loss. The order matters, and the first step is not yours to rush. Water in a low space can hide electrical, gas and contamination hazards.

Safety before anything else

Do not walk into standing water

Water below grade can reach outlets, appliances, a panel or a furnace. If the water is above outlet height or touching any electrical equipment, stay out. Cut power at the main only if the panel is dry and reachable without wading. If it is not, call the utility or 911 before you call us.

If you smell gas, leave and call the gas utility or 911. Never run a gasoline pump or generator indoors, because exhaust contains carbon monoxide.

Floodwater and sewage backup can carry bacteria. Avoid skin contact, keep children and pets out, and do not use household fans to dry it before the source is identified.

While you wait for the crew, keep your actions simple and safe. Photograph the water line on walls and stored items from the doorway. Move anything you can lift from upper stairs and dry floors, and take medications, documents and valuables out of the house. Write down when you first noticed the water and what you think caused it.

What flood cleanup services actually do

1

Assess and shut off the source

The crew checks where the water came from, whether it is still entering and whether utilities are safe.

2

Pump and extract

Truck-mounted or portable pumps and extraction units remove standing water, usually in stages so structure is not stressed.

3

Remove what cannot be saved

Carpet, pad, wet drywall, insulation and soaked belongings are cut out and bagged, especially after contaminated water.

4

Dry the structure

Air movers and LGR dehumidifiers run until moisture meters show normal readings in framing and concrete.

5

Clean and sanitize

Surfaces are cleaned and treated, and odor is addressed once drying is under way.

6

Document and plan rebuild

Photos, readings and an inventory support insurance conversations and the rebuild scope.

Where the water came from changes the plan

Clean supply water from a broken line is treated differently from rainwater entering through a window well, and both differ from sewage backing up through a floor drain. Industry standard ANSI/IICRC S500 sorts water into categories, and the category sets what can be dried and what must be thrown out.

  • Supply line or water heater failure: usually the cleanest category, though it degrades with time.
  • Groundwater or storm entry: treated as contaminated, so porous materials are normally removed.
  • Sewage backup: contaminated from the start. Do not clean it yourself.
  • Failed sump pump: depends on what the water picked up on its way in.

Ask any company whether its crews work to S500 and how it classified your loss. A clear answer is a good sign.

What can usually be saved and what usually cannot

Salvage rules of thumb after flood contamination

  • ✓Often saved: concrete slabs, solid wood framing, metal and hard plastic items that can be cleaned
  • ✓Often removed: carpet and pad, drywall, insulation, particle board, upholstered furniture
  • ✓Case by case: hardwood, solid wood furniture, documents and photos (some can be professionally dried)
  • ✓Never reused: food, medicine and anything absorbent that sat in sewage

Remove ruined belongings only after you have photographed them. If the water was contaminated, our water damage cleanup service handles the sanitizing and odor control once the space is dry. The first pumping and extraction step is covered under emergency water removal.

A working sump pump is not the end of the story. If the pump failed, the float stuck or the discharge line clogged, the same water can return after cleanup. Have the pump, check valve and discharge line tested as part of the rebuild plan, and ask the crew to note where water entered so the repair addresses the entry point.

Why speed matters below grade

Low spaces dry slowly because air does not move well and concrete holds water. The EPA advises drying or discarding wet materials within 24 to 48 hours to limit mold growth, which is hard to meet without equipment. Early water mitigation with a dehumidifier sized to the space is what keeps a wet basement from turning into a mold job.

Insurance and flood coverage in South Gate

Flood, insurance and South Gate

The Los Angeles River runs through the eastern part of South Gate. Check the FEMA Flood Map Service Center for your address and ask your insurer whether you have flood coverage, because a map reading does not tell you what your policy pays.

The California Department of Insurance says homeowners policies typically exclude flood, mudslide and debris flow. Flood coverage comes from the National Flood Insurance Program, whose policies generally take effect 30 days after purchase, so buying one after a storm is forecast is too late. The CDI hotline is 1-800-927-4357 and the NFIP claims line is 1-800-638-6620.

Rebuilding below grade can involve plumbing, electrical and structure. South Gate Building and Safety, at 8650 California Avenue and (323) 563-9549, says permits are typically required for those, so ask before the rebuild starts.
