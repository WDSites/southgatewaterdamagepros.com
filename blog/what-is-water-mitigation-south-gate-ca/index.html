What Is Water Mitigation? The First 72 Hours Explained

Water mitigation is the work that stops water damage from getting worse: finding the source, removing standing water and drying the structure. Restoration and rebuilding come after it.

☎ Call (213) 776-0579

By the South Gate Water Damage Pros team · 2026

Water mitigation means limiting the damage a water loss can do. It covers stopping the source, extracting standing water, removing materials that cannot be saved, and drying everything else with air movers and dehumidifiers until moisture meters show normal readings. It is the first phase of the job, and it usually decides how big the second phase gets.

Mitigation, restoration and repair compared

Three phases of a water loss
PhaseMain goalTypical workWho is involved
MitigationStop further damageSource control, water extraction, tear-out of wet materials, drying, monitoringMitigation crew, plumber if a pipe failed
RestorationReturn the property to its prior conditionCleaning, sanitizing, deodorizing, content handling, final checksRestoration crew
Repair and rebuildReplace what was removedDrywall, insulation, flooring, baseboard, paintContractor, with permits where needed

Companies often use these words loosely, so ask for the scope in writing. A quote that says only "water damage services" does not tell you which phase you are buying.

Hours 0 to 24: stop, assess and remove

The first day is about control. The crew finds where the water came from, confirms it is shut off, and checks for electrical and slip hazards. Then they sort out how far the water traveled, using moisture meters and a thermal imaging camera to see wet areas behind walls and under floors that look dry.

Standing water comes out next with extraction units. Every hour water sits, it wicks further into drywall, baseboard and subfloor, so extraction is the step that saves the most material. If you have an active leak or a flooded room, emergency water removal is the part of mitigation that cannot wait.

Hours 24 to 72: tear-out and structured drying

Once the bulk water is gone, the crew decides what to remove. Soaked insulation, wet carpet pad and drywall that has lost strength usually go, because they dry slowly and can hold moisture against framing. Cutting wall base a few inches up or drilling small holes lets air reach the cavity.

Then drying equipment goes in. Air movers push dry air across surfaces, and LGR dehumidifiers pull moisture out of the air so it does not settle somewhere else. The crew returns to take readings, usually daily, and moves equipment as wet areas shrink. Drying is finished when readings match an unaffected reference, not when the room feels dry.

A good crew also documents the work. Expect photos of the damage before tear-out, a sketch of the affected rooms, the starting moisture readings and a log of equipment placed and removed. That paper trail helps you compare quotes and gives your insurer a clear record if you file a claim.

What the equipment does

Core mitigation equipment and its job

  • ✓Extraction units: pull standing water from floors and carpet
  • ✓Moisture meters: measure moisture inside wood, drywall and concrete
  • ✓Thermal imaging camera: shows temperature patterns that point to hidden wet areas
  • ✓Air movers: speed evaporation from surfaces and open cavities
  • ✓LGR dehumidifiers: remove the moisture that evaporation puts into the air
  • ✓Drying log: daily readings that show the job is actually progressing

Why water category changes the plan

Professional standards, such as ANSI/IICRC S500, sort water into categories by how contaminated it is. Clean supply water from a broken line is treated differently from gray water from a washer, and both differ from sewage or floodwater, which are contaminated from the start.

The category affects what can be kept. Porous materials soaked with contaminated water, like carpet pad and drywall, are usually removed rather than dried. Clean water gives a better chance of saving them. Any company you hire should be willing to say whether its crews work to S500 and explain how it classified your loss.

Time matters too. Clean water left alone can degrade in condition, and the EPA advises drying or discarding wet materials within 24 to 48 hours to limit mold growth. That is the real reason mitigation starts before the insurance conversation is finished.

An open window is not a substitute for equipment. When the outside air is humid, it carries moisture in, and a single household fan cannot lower humidity in a closed, wet room. Controlled drying keeps the air dry and moving at the same time.

What you can do before the crew arrives

1

Stop the source

Shut the main or fixture valve. If you cannot, call your water provider's emergency line.

2

Protect the electrical

Cut power only if the panel is dry and safe to reach. Otherwise call the utility or 911 and stay out.

3

Photograph everything

Take wide and close photos of the water, the source and damaged items before anything moves.

4

Lift what you safely can

Move small furniture, rugs and electronics out of the wet area. Do not lift heavy items alone.

5

Leave ruined materials in place

Do not rip out drywall or flooring before the crew sees it, because they need to document it.

For cleaning, sanitizing and odor control once the drying is complete, see our water damage cleanup service. If walls, floors or ceilings were opened, rebuilding falls under water damage repair.
