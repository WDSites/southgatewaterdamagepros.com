Why a failed supply line floods so fast

When a pressurized supply line, fitting or connector fails, water sprays or pours until someone closes the main valve. Our 24/7 line at (213) 776-0579 sends a crew to extract the water and dry the structure, while a licensed plumber repairs the pipe. Burst pipe emergency: what to do right now

  1. Close the main shutoff valve fully. It sits where the supply line enters the house or beside the water meter.
  2. Open a faucet at the lowest point in the home to relieve pressure and drain the line.
  3. Look for water near outlets, light fixtures or the panel before you walk through it.
  4. Lift rugs, electronics and small furniture out of the wet area.
  5. Take dated photos and a short video of the pipe, the water and the damage before cleanup begins.
  6. Call a licensed plumber, then call us at (213) 776-0579. A crew heads out the same day when one can be spared.

Safety in the first hour: power, valves and ceilings

Water and electricity do not mix

If water is reaching outlets, a panel or wiring, do not step into it. Cut power at the panel only if you can reach it from dry ground. For any gas smell or electrical risk, call the utility or 911 before you call us.

A ceiling under an upstairs break can hold a lot of water and drop without warning. Stay out from under a bulging or sagging ceiling and keep children and pets in another room.

Where the water goes after a pipe lets go

Water follows gravity and the easiest path. Inside a wall it runs down along the studs, pools on the bottom plate and wicks up through the drywall. From there it enters the subfloor, travels sideways under flooring and drops through any opening to the room below.

That is why a dry-looking floor can sit on a wet subfloor. A thermal imaging camera and a moisture meter show the wet areas, so the crew opens only what has to open.

How we extract and dry after a pipe failure

Extraction comes first. Extraction units and wands pull standing water out of carpet and hard floors, and a submersible pump handles deeper pooling. It is the same first step described on our emergency water removal page.

Equipment on a burst pipe job

  • ✓Extraction units and wands for floors and carpet
  • ✓Moisture meters for drywall, framing and subfloor
  • ✓Thermal imaging camera to trace wet cavities
  • ✓Air movers to push dry air across wet surfaces
  • ✓LGR dehumidifiers to pull moisture out of the air
  • ✓Wall cavity drying through small holes or removed baseboards, only where readings call for it

Insulation and studs dry slowly behind closed drywall, so the crew may pull baseboards or drill small relief holes. Our structural drying page explains setpoints and daily readings. ANSI/IICRC S500 is the industry's published guide to professional water damage work, and you can ask any company how it applies it.

Who does what: your plumber and our crew

A licensed plumber finds the break and repairs or replaces the pipe. We do not repair plumbing. We extract, dry, clean and coordinate the rebuild, and we can work alongside the plumber.

After the pipe is fixed and tested, drywall, flooring and paint are rebuilt once readings show the structure is dry, as covered on our water damage repair page. Wet materials we remove are handled as described under water damage cleanup.

What changes the cost of burst pipe cleanup

  • How long the water ran before the main was closed
  • How many rooms and floor levels the water reached
  • Flooring type, since carpet pad, hardwood and tile behave differently
  • Whether water got into wall cavities, insulation or ceilings below
  • How many days of equipment and monitoring visits the readings call for

Insurance notes and mistakes to avoid

Sudden and accidental pipe failure is often covered, while slow seepage and neglect are often excluded. Check your policy or ask your insurer, because we cannot promise what any policy pays. Dated photos, moisture readings and the plumber's invoice help show what happened.

  • Waiting to see whether the leak stops on its own
  • Running only a household fan, which leaves wall cavities wet
  • Painting over a stain before the wall reads dry
