A mostly rented city: water damage and your landlord

About 73.4 percent of Huntington Park's occupied homes are renter-occupied, a share that shapes what happens after a leak. The landlord or property manager owns the building's plumbing and structure, and the tenant owns their belongings, so the first calls are usually to the property manager and to a renters insurer.

The ANSI/IICRC S500 industry standard guides how a Huntington Park crew classifies water and decides when a building is dry enough to close up.

If you rent, send your landlord written notice with dated photos the same day, keep a copy and ask for confirmation. If you own a unit in a multi-family building, tell the association too. Then call our 24/7 line so drying can begin while paperwork is sorted out.

When water is spreading in Huntington Park

In apartment buildings, water rarely stays in one unit. It drops through ceilings to the unit below and runs along walls to the unit beside you. Call as soon as you see it, name your unit and floor, and ask whether neighbors below are affected. Our line answers all night, and crews head out as soon as one is available.

A streetcar suburb with 1930s storefronts

Huntington Park began as a streetcar suburb and incorporated in 1906. Pacific Boulevard, its main commercial strip, carries a 1930s shopping district and Art Deco theaters, including the Warner Theater, which opened in 1930. Buildings from that period often have original plumbing, shared walls and old roofs, and a leak in one storefront can reach the next.

Business owners there should photograph the damage, notify the landlord, and call their insurer about business interruption coverage. A crew can log what was removed and what readings were taken, which is the paper trail an adjuster asks for.

Why an apartment leak needs a different plan

Drying a single house is straightforward compared with drying a building. Water from one unit enters the ceiling cavity of the unit below, follows pipes through shared chases and can reach hallway carpet. A crew checks the affected neighbors with a moisture meter, since a wall can read wet well away from the visible stain.

Access also takes coordination. Crews need the manager to open units, equipment has to be placed where residents can still walk safely and cords must stay clear of wet floors. Tell the dispatcher how many units you think are affected so the crew arrives with enough equipment for the whole building.

If water is still spreading: before we arrive

1

Name the source

Close the fixture valve or the main valve. In an apartment, ask the manager where the building shutoff is.

2

Stay clear of power

Keep away from wet outlets and panels, and call the utility or 911 for any electrical or gas risk.

3

Save what is dry

Move papers, electronics and bedding to a dry room before they wick up water.

4

Photograph first

Take dated photos of every wet room and every damaged item before anything moves.
