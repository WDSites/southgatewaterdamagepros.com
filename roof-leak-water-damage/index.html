Why roof leaks show up after the first big storm

A roof leak is rain that gets past shingles, tile, flashing or a seam and ends up inside as stained ceilings, wet attic insulation and dripping light fixtures. This page covers the cleanup and drying. A licensed roofer finds and closes the opening, and we handle the water damage and can work alongside that roofer.

Rain in the Los Angeles area comes in a few storms instead of steady weather. A small leak can sit unnoticed through the dry months, so many roof leaks are found only after the first heavy rain of the season. By then the insulation above your ceiling may have been wet for hours.

Roof leak emergency: what to do right now

Call the 24/7 line at (213) 776-0579 while you work through these steps, and see emergency water removal if water is also pooling on the floor. A ceiling holding water can drop without warning, so keep people out from under it.

  1. Move people and pets away from any bulging or sagging ceiling.
  2. Set a bucket under the drip and cover furniture you cannot move with plastic.
  3. Call a roofer for a temporary tarp. Do not climb a wet roof yourself.

Electrical and ceiling safety before you touch anything

Water and wiring share the ceiling cavity in most homes. If water is dripping from a fixture, do not switch it on. Turn off that room's breaker only if the panel is dry and you can reach it while standing on a dry floor.

When to stay out and call someone else first

If you see sparks, smell burning or find water near the electrical panel, stay out of the area. Call the utility or 911 before you call us.

Do not poke, press or cut a bulging ceiling to let it drain. The board and the water above it can fall together, so leave that to a trained crew.

How we dry ceilings, attic space and wall tops

Once the roof is covered, the work moves inside. We use moisture meters and a thermal imaging camera to map how far the water traveled, because it follows rafters and wall framing in ways the stain does not show.

What the crew does on a roof leak job

  • ✓Removes wet ceiling insulation and sagging drywall that is holding water
  • ✓Extracts water from carpet and floors beneath the leak with extraction units
  • ✓Sets air movers and LGR dehumidifiers to dry joists, wall tops and remaining drywall
  • ✓Reads moisture levels on each visit and keeps dated photos for your claim
  • ✓Applies antimicrobial treatment where materials stayed wet, with mold remediation if growth is visible

EPA mold guidance says wet materials should be dried or thrown out within one to two days. That window is why wet attic insulation should not wait for the weekend, and it is the reason speed decides what is saved. Replacing the board, texture and paint afterward follows the steps on our drywall water damage repair page.

Roofer first, restoration second

We do not repair roofs. A licensed roofer finds the entry point, whether that is a lifted shingle, a cracked tile, failed flashing around a vent or a clogged valley, and makes the repair. We extract, dry, clean and coordinate the rebuild, so a temporary tarp can go up while our drying equipment runs. The first-hours steps are on our water mitigation page.

A wind-driven storm with several openings is covered on our storm water damage page. Tenants in Huntington Park or Paramount who see a stained ceiling should tell the landlord in writing the same day, since the roof belongs to the building owner.

What changes the cost of a roof leak cleanup

  • How many rooms and how much attic area the water reached
  • Whether insulation and ceiling board must come out or can dry in place
  • Water category, since rain that crossed a dirty attic is not always clean
  • How much rebuilding follows, such as drywall, texture, paint and flooring

Rain damage and your policy

Sudden and accidental damage, such as a storm tearing open a roof, is often covered. Leaks from worn roofing that went ignored are often not. Check your policy or ask your insurer before you decide anything, and keep photos of the roof, the stains and the repair invoice. ANSI/IICRC S500 is the published standard behind professional drying and cleanup.
