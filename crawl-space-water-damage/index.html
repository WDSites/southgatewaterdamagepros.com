Does your South Gate home have a crawl space?

Crawl space water damage means water has collected or leaked under the floor of a house on a raised foundation. We pump out standing water, remove soaked insulation and dry the joists and subfloor above. The 24/7 line is answered around the clock, so call (213) 776-0579 as soon as you see water.

Not every South Gate home has a crawl space. Many are slab-on-grade, and we cannot say how many are which, so check your foundation type. Look for small vents along the outer wall or an access hatch in a closet or hallway floor. Slab homes are covered on the slab leak water damage page.

Safety before anyone goes under the house

Stay out of a wet crawl space

Do not crawl into a space with standing water. Wiring, junction boxes and sometimes a furnace or ducts run under many floors, and water plus electricity can seriously hurt you. Cut power only if the panel is dry and safe to reach, and call the utility or 911 first for a gas smell or any electrical risk.

If the water looks dark or smells like sewage, keep everyone out of the house area above it and tell us when you call.

How the crew pumps, strips and dries under the floor

The crew checks access and hazards first. Submersible pumps move standing water out, then extraction units and wet vacuums pull what remains from the soil surface and the wood. Our emergency water removal page describes the pumping step in more detail.

Insulation that has soaked up water is bagged and removed, because it holds moisture against the subfloor and dries slowly. Moisture meters check the joists and subfloor, and a thermal imaging camera helps trace wet areas above the floor. Air movers and LGR dehumidifiers then dry the framing, and drying mats can draw moisture from wood flooring above.

The EPA's mold guidance puts the deadline at one to two days for drying or discarding wet materials, which makes the first day under the house the one that counts. Where growth is already visible on wood, the mold remediation page explains that separate service.

ANSI/IICRC S500 is the standard restorers publish their water damage practice against, and you can ask any company how it follows it. Drying is finished when readings match those of unaffected materials, as explained on the structural drying page, not when the floor feels dry.

What changes the cost of a crawl space job

  • Depth of standing water and how much of the under-floor area it covers
  • Access: hatch size, clearance height and distance to the wet area
  • Water source and category, clean supply water or contaminated
  • Amount of insulation and plastic sheeting that must come out
  • Drying equipment needed and the number of monitoring days
  • Whether joists or subfloor show damage that needs a rebuild

How insurance usually treats water under the house

Sudden and accidental damage, such as a pipe that fails without warning, is often covered by a homeowners policy. Gradual leaks and neglect often are not, and flood is usually excluded. Check your policy or ask your insurer, and keep dated photos and moisture readings ready.

Mistakes that keep a crawl space wet

  • Waiting to see whether the water drains away on its own
  • Sealing the vents or hatch while the wood is still wet
  • Leaving soaked insulation hanging from the joists
  • Pulling insulation out without gloves and a respirator
  • Masking the smell with air freshener instead of finding the wet material
