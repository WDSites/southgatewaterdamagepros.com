When wet carpet can be saved and when it cannot

Wet carpet can often be dried in place when the water was clean and extraction started quickly. It has to come out when the water was contaminated or sat too long. Our crews use extraction wands, lift the carpet and dry the pad area and subfloor, and explain what can stay before work starts. The deciding factors are the water source and the hours since it spread.

Carpet sits on a cushion pad, held at the walls by tack strips, over a wood subfloor or a concrete slab. Water passes through the top layer in seconds and the pad soaks it up like a sponge, so a damp-feeling surface can hide a saturated pad.

Why the water category decides the plan

ANSI/IICRC S500 is a published standard that restorers use for water damage work, and it sorts water into three categories by how contaminated it is. The category sets what happens to your carpet.

Water source and the usual outcome for carpet
Water sourceCategoryUsual outcome for carpet and pad
Broken supply line or clean tap waterCategory 1, cleanCarpet often dried in place if caught fast, pad often replaced
Washer or dishwasher overflow with soap and dirtCategory 2, grayCarpet may be cleaned and dried if acted on quickly, pad removed
Sewage or surface flood waterCategory 3, contaminatedCarpet and pad removed and discarded, see sewage backup cleanup

Pad is the layer most often replaced, even when the carpet stays, because it holds water and dries slowly.

Safety before you step on soaked carpet

Wet floors, power and unknown water

If water is near outlets, power strips, a panel or wiring, stay out of it. Cut power at the panel only if you can reach it from dry ground. For a gas smell or any electrical risk, call the utility or 911 first.

If the water came from a toilet, drain or sewer line, treat the carpet as contaminated and keep children and pets off it.

The 24 to 48 hour clock

Under EPA mold guidance, wet carpet that stays wet beyond roughly two days becomes a mold risk. For carpet, that clock matters because the pad and the underside of the carpet stay wet long after the top feels dry. The faster the water is extracted, the more of the floor can be saved.

If growth shows up, ask about mold remediation, where ANSI/IICRC S520 is the published reference.

How we extract, lift and dry wet carpet

We start with extraction wands and an extraction unit to pull water out of the fibers and the pad. A moisture meter and a thermal imaging camera then show how far the water went. If the carpet can be saved, it is lifted at a wall, released from the tack strips, supported so air moves under it, and the pad is cut out and removed.

Equipment on a wet carpet job

  • ✓Extraction wands and units for fibers and pad
  • ✓Moisture meters for subfloor and concrete slab readings
  • ✓Air movers aimed under and across lifted carpet
  • ✓LGR dehumidifiers to pull moisture from the room air
  • ✓Thermal imaging camera to find hidden wet patches

Under the pad, a wood subfloor or a concrete slab can hold moisture for days. Readings from that surface tell the crew when the carpet can go back down on fresh pad. Drying time depends on the materials and the readings, not on a fixed number of days. Our structural drying page explains how those readings and daily logs guide the work.

What a wet vac cannot do

A rented wet vac lifts surface water but cannot pull water out of a thick pad or reach the subfloor under it.

  • Leaving the pad in place because the top feels dry
  • Walking on and dragging furniture across soaked carpet
  • Waiting through a weekend to see if it dries by itself

What changes the cost of wet carpet work

  • Water category and how long the carpet stayed wet
  • How many rooms are wet
  • Whether the carpet can stay or must be replaced
  • Days of equipment the subfloor or slab readings call for
  • New carpet, pad and installation if replacement is needed

Replacement carpet and installation are rebuild work that we coordinate after drying, and wet material removal follows our water damage cleanup process. We explain the scope before work starts.

Carpet claims and your policy

Sudden and accidental water, such as a failed supply line, is often covered. Gradual leaks and neglect are often not, and flood water is usually excluded from homeowners policies. Check your policy or ask your insurer, and keep your photos, notes on where the water came from and any receipts.
