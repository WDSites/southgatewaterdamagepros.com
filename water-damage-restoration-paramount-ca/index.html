Paramount at a glance

Paramount quick facts

Los Angeles County city incorporated in 1957, with about 53,733 residents in the 2020 census. ZIP code 90723 is covered, and emergency dispatch is available around the clock.

Paramount Boulevard, the city's main north-south street, gave the city its name, and Interstate 105 crosses the northern part of town. Give the dispatcher the nearest cross street to Paramount Boulevard or the side of I-105 you are on, which speeds up routing.

A city of renters and owners: who to notify

About 57.1 percent of Paramount's occupied homes are renter-occupied. Tenants should notify the landlord in writing with dated photos and keep a copy, while owners should call their insurer early. Both should keep receipts for emergency costs, such as a hotel or purchased supplies.

The city was incorporated in 1957 during the postwar building era, so many homes there date from the decades after. Homes from that period commonly have copper supply lines and cast-iron drains, so tell the crew roughly when your house was built if a pipe has failed.

Paperwork after a Paramount leak

Sudden and accidental water damage, such as a failed supply line, is often covered by homeowners and renters policies. Gradual leaks and neglect often are not, and flood is usually excluded. Read your policy for the deductible and the reporting deadline, then call your insurer before large repairs begin.

Keep a folder of dated photos, the crew's moisture readings, receipts and the written scope. If you disagree with an insurer's decision, the California Department of Insurance consumer hotline is 1-800-927-4357. We document the loss, but the coverage decision belongs to your insurer.

Mistakes that make Paramount water losses worse

  • Waiting for the stain to dry before calling, which leaves moisture in the wall
  • Running a household vacuum over wet carpet or standing water
  • Turning the air conditioning on high to dry the room without a plan
  • Painting over a stain before the wall is confirmed dry
