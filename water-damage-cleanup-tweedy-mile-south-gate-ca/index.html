Water Damage Cleanup in Tweedy Mile, South Gate, CA

Along the Tweedy Mile, shops, restaurants and homes share walls and plumbing, so one leak can reach a neighbor before anyone finds it.

The Tweedy Mile and the blocks around it

The City of South Gate's website describes the Tweedy Mile as the place to sample local shops and restaurants. The name comes from the Tweedy family. The city says R.D. Tweedy, who moved to California in 1852, bought 2,000 acres, and much of South Gate was later built on that land.

The city's zoning map marks a Tweedy Boulevard Specific Plan Area, and the Housing Element lists the Tweedy Boulevard Specific Plan among the city's plans. The General Plan also names a Tweedy Educational District. We did not find a published boundary for the Tweedy Mile, so give your street address when you call and we will confirm service.

Shared walls, shared pipes

On a business corridor, one tenant's leak can travel through a shared wall, ceiling or floor into the next unit. A sink line, an upstairs pipe or a roof drain can put water where the tenant below cannot reach the shutoff. Tell the landlord or property manager at once, and call the neighbors you share a wall with.

Citywide figures from the Housing Element show why this matters beyond shops. About 28 percent of South Gate homes are in multifamily buildings, and 58 percent of occupied homes are rented. Those are citywide numbers, not Tweedy Mile counts, but renters near the corridor should expect to involve a landlord.

Before you call

Find out who controls the main shutoff, since in a multi-tenant building it is often the landlord's. Photograph the damage and the shutoff location first, and read your lease and policy before cleanup starts.

Flood and storm questions

We did not find a published FEMA flood-zone status for the Tweedy Mile area. Neighborhood-specific FEMA flood-zone status not verified, and flood zones are mapped by area, so check your own address on the FEMA Flood Map Service Center. Zone X does not mean flooding cannot happen.

Treat water that came in from outside or up through a drain as contaminated. Keep people away, avoid wading through it and call before you start cleaning.

First-hour safety in a shop or apartment

1

Find the shutoff

Look for the main valve or a fixture valve, and ask the landlord if it is not in your unit. Do not stand in water to reach it.

2

Keep electricity away from water

Stay off wet floors near outlets, cords and equipment. If the panel is in a wet area, call the utility or 911 first.

3

Clear the wet area

Keep customers, residents and pets out of wet or slippery areas, and out of dark or foul water.

4

Document before moving anything

Take dated photos, list damaged stock or belongings and note who you told and when.

Drying across units

Moisture can move into the wall or floor of the unit beside the wet one, so readings are taken beyond the visibly wet area. Standing water is removed first, then air movers and dehumidifiers run until the wet materials read like dry ones. ANSI/IICRC S500 is the industry standard we use as a reference for classifying water and judging drying.

Closing a wall too early can trap moisture. If a room smells musty after several days, see the mold remediation page for the next step.

Lease and insurance paperwork

Photos, moisture readings and an itemized scope help describe the loss to a landlord or insurer. Who pays depends on the cause, the lease and each policy, and we cannot decide that for you. Ask your insurer and your landlord early, and keep copies of every notice you send.
