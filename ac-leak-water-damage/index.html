Where AC water ends up in a South Gate home

An air conditioner pulls moisture out of the air as it cools, and that water has to leave the house. It runs into a drain pan, down a condensate line and outside. When the line clogs with algae and dust, the pan overflows into the ceiling, attic insulation or wall below. We dry and repair that damage, and a licensed HVAC technician fixes the unit.

Air conditioning runs a long season in Southern California, so condensate leaks are common and usually slow. A drip that starts in June can stain a ceiling by September.

Safety first when AC water is near wiring

An air conditioner is a wired electrical appliance, and the water that escapes it often drips through ceiling cavities that hold light fixtures and wiring. Turn cooling off at the thermostat first. Cut the breaker for the unit only if the panel is dry and you can reach it standing on dry floor.

Wet fixtures and sagging ceilings

If water drips from a ceiling light, a fan or an outlet, stay away from it. Turn off that circuit only if the panel is dry and safe to reach, and call an electrician. For sparking, a burning smell or a gas odor, call the utility or 911 before you call us.

A bulging ceiling can drop water and wet insulation at once. Keep people out from under it until the crew can release the water in a controlled way.

Who fixes the unit and who fixes the damage

We are a water damage restoration company. We do not repair air conditioners, clear condensate lines or replace drain pans. A licensed HVAC technician does that work, and the leak returns if the cause is left alone. We can work alongside the technician.

How we dry out an AC leak

The crew starts with a moisture meter and a thermal imaging camera, because a stained ceiling often hides a larger wet area. Readings are taken on the stain, around it and on dry materials elsewhere in the house. The ANSI/IICRC S500 standard is the written benchmark for restoring water-damaged buildings, and you can ask any company how it uses it.

Gradual AC leaks and your insurance

Insurers usually separate sudden and accidental damage from gradual leaks. A pan that cracked and flooded a ceiling overnight may be treated differently from a drain line that dripped for months. Gradual seepage is often excluded.

Check your policy or ask your insurer before you assume either way. We keep dated photos and moisture readings for your claim, and renters should tell the landlord in writing.

Mold risk and habits that prevent the next leak

Wet insulation and drywall that stay damp invite mold, and AC leaks often run unseen for weeks. If you see dark spotting or smell mustiness, the mold remediation page explains how that work is handled. ANSI/IICRC S520 is the published reference for mold remediation, and you can ask any company about it.

  • Ask your HVAC technician to flush the condensate line at each service visit
  • Check the pan under an attic air handler every spring
  • Ask about a float switch that shuts the unit off when the pan fills
