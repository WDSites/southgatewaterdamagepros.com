What a commercial water loss involves

A commercial water loss is water damage in a space where people work, sell or store goods, and it differs from a house loss in who is affected. Water leaves one unit and enters another, the building owner holds part of the structure, and every day closed costs income. We extract the water, dry the structure, clean the space and coordinate the rebuild, working with your plumber, roofer or electrician for the cause.

Commercial spaces also have features that slow drying: suspended ceilings, carpet tile, shared walls, storage racks and long runs of drywall partitions. The ANSI/IICRC S500 standard, the published benchmark for professional water damage work in buildings, guides how water is classified and dried.

Safety first: the first hour at a flooded business

  • Get everyone out of any area with standing water near outlets, panels or equipment.
  • Shut off the water at the main or at the fixture, if you can reach the valve safely.
  • Cut power to the wet area only if the panel is dry and you can reach it on dry ground.
  • Call the utility or 911 first for any gas or electrical risk, including sprinkler lines near live wiring.
  • Keep staff and customers out of dark, foul water until it is identified.

Our process on a shop, office or shared building

The crew starts with a walkthrough and moisture readings, then removes standing water with pumps and extraction units, as in our emergency water removal work. A thermal imaging camera and moisture meters find wet insulation, ceiling tiles and wall bases that look dry. Air movers and LGR dehumidifiers dry the structure, and readings are logged each visit.

Wet materials that cannot be saved come out, and surfaces are cleaned. Our water damage cleanup page covers that stage.

We do not repair plumbing, roofs, sprinkler systems or building equipment. A licensed plumber, roofer or fire sprinkler contractor fixes the cause, and we can work alongside them.

Documenting the loss for landlord and insurer

Take photos and short video before anything is moved, and keep a running list of damaged inventory, equipment and supplies with approximate values. Insurance questions for a business water loss

Sudden and accidental damage, such as a burst supply line, is often covered by a commercial property policy, while slow leaks and neglected maintenance often are not. Flood is usually excluded and needs separate flood coverage. Business interruption coverage is its own question, so ask your insurer what it requires. Check your policy before assuming anything.

What changes the cost of a commercial job

  • Area of the loss and how many units or rooms it reached
  • Water category and how long it sat before extraction
  • Floor and ceiling types, such as carpet tile, suspended ceiling or sealed concrete
  • Shared walls that force drying in a neighboring space
  • Wet stock and fixtures that must be moved or removed
  • Equipment count and monitoring days

Tenants, landlords and neighbors

Who pays for what depends on your lease and policies, not on us. As a tenant, report the loss to your landlord in writing right away. As a landlord, tell the tenants next door. Many people rent their workspace in Huntington Park and Paramount, where tenant and landlord notice comes up often.
